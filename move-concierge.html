Who it’s for

Independent moving companies with a busy phone line, where the owner, general manager or office manager decides how calls are handled and there’s already a phone, a calendar and a CRM or dispatch tool in place. It works for a single location as well as several.

The problem

Moving customers call in the evening, on weekends, and in the middle of the day when everyone is on another line. By the time someone calls back in the morning, the customer may already have booked an estimate elsewhere.

How it works, from the call to your team

  1. The call comes in

    On the calls you route to it: after hours, on weekends, or when every line is busy. Callers talk normally and can interrupt.

  2. It asks about the move

    Moving date, where from and where to, the size of the home, stairs and elevator access, and large or fragile items such as a piano, artwork, gym equipment or a safe. It doesn’t give binding prices or promise dates.

  3. It writes up the lead

    Contact details and every answer go into one record, with the call recording and transcript attached.

  4. It books the next step

    Where you’ve set it up, it books a virtual or in-home estimate in your estimators’ calendar, or schedules a callback, and sends the caller a summary by text message.

  5. Your team takes it from there

    The lead reaches your staff by email or in your moving software or CRM, depending on what we connect at setup. Complaints, unusual requests and anything it can’t answer go straight to a person.

What you can see

Every call is recorded and transcribed, each lead has a lead score, and a daily summary shows leads, bookings and the callers who still need a call back.

What it won’t doIt won’t quote a binding price, promise a date or agree to an exception. Those go to your team.
